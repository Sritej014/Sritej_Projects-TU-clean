This project analyzed the sales database of a model vehicle company to answer three important business questions: which products should be restocked, which customers should receive more marketing attention, and how much the company can afford to spend on acquiring new customers.

The first step was to analyze product performance and inventory levels. By comparing the quantity sold with the quantity currently in stock and calculating the revenue generated by each product, we identified products that were both popular and running relatively low on inventory. Vintage Cars and Motorcycles appeared frequently among the highest-priority products, suggesting that these product lines deserve particular attention when planning future inventory purchases.

Next, customer profitability was analyzed by calculating the profit generated by every customer. This made it possible to identify the top five VIP customers and the five least engaged customers. VIP customers can be rewarded through loyalty programs and personalized offers, while customers generating relatively little profit could be targeted through discounts or special campaigns designed to increase engagement.

Finally, we calculated the average customer lifetime value, which was approximately $39,040 in profit. This metric provides a useful reference point for customer acquisition decisions because it estimates how much profit a new customer may generate over time.

Through this project, SQL techniques such as joins, aggregate functions, grouping, common table expressions, views, sorting, and filtering were used to transform raw transactional data into actionable business insights.
